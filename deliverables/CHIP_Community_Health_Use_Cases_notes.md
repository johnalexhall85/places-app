# CHIP Community Health Use Cases — slide notes

This five-slide deck is intentionally hypothetical. It uses data and product views already present in CHIP, but it does not invent findings, rankings, or dollar amounts.

## Screenshot replacement guide

1. **Framing slide:** Use a clean CHIP hero view showing the national or regional map with one selected-geography profile panel.
2. **Local health departments:** Select a county or tract and show a PLACES measure with a profile panel; if useful, capture a second state with the SVI, HPSA, or FEMA selector visible.
3. **Nonprofits:** Show a USDA Food Access or Food Environment layer for a candidate service area, with the geography profile or map detail visible.
4. **Policymakers:** Show a FEMA NRI hazard layer for a selected county. If space permits, pair it with a PLACES, CMS, HPSA, or SVI profile for the same place to demonstrate how hazard data complements public-health context.
5. **Lawmakers:** Show a county or tract profile, or an Ask CHIP response, with the underlying source and methodology context visible.

To replace a placeholder in PowerPoint, insert and crop the screenshot over the inner map/profile wireframe while leaving the dashed outer frame and caption in place. The screenshot will sit in front of the wireframe elements, so no deletion is required.

## Scenario logic

- **Local health departments:** prioritize outreach or response where multiple public-health signals overlap.
- **Nonprofits:** choose service areas, operating partners, and baseline measures.
- **Policymakers:** combine HHS public-health signals with FEMA hazard context to strengthen preparedness and continuity planning.
- **Lawmakers:** use place-based public-health evidence to sharpen hearings, legislation, reporting requirements, and measures of progress.

## Interpretation guardrail

CHIP combines modeled indicators and public administrative records. Measures should be interpreted with the cited source, geography, period, and methodology. FEMA NRI is a complementary planning layer: it adds hazard and resilience context but does not replace HHS health, provider-access, or population measures. Overlapping health, access, vulnerability, and hazard patterns can guide questions and planning; they do not establish causal impact by themselves.
